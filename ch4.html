    4.1Kinematic configuration

    The pose: three numbers for a planar robot

    The kinematic configuration (or kinematic state) of a rigid mobile robot in a planar environment is its pose, the position and orientation relative to a global frame:

    xt=(xyθ).x_t = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ \theta \end{pmatrix}.xt​=​xyθ​​.

    Review question 2 asks you to illustrate this model. Six variables are needed in 3D (three Cartesian, three Euler angles), but a robot on a flat floor has only three. The location is (x,y)(x, y)(x,y); the bearing or heading is θ\thetaθ. A pose with θ=0\theta = 0θ=0 points along the global x-axis.

    Kinematics, the calculus describing the effect of controls on configuration, is deterministic in Chapter 1. Here it becomes probabilistic: the outcome of a control is a distribution.

    From deterministic to probabilistic kinematics

    Chapter 1 gave x˙=vcos⁡θ, y˙=vsin⁡θ, θ˙=ω\dot x = v\cos\theta,\ \dot y = v\sin\theta,\ \dot\theta = \omegax˙=vcosθ, y˙​=vsinθ, θ˙=ω, a deterministic function. In reality the same command applied twice gives two different poses. Probabilistic kinematics replaces the function with the motion model

    p(xt∣ut, xt−1),p(x_t \mid u_t,\ x_{t-1}),p(xt​∣ut​, xt−1​),

    the posterior over the successor pose given the previous pose and the control. It is the state-transition distribution of the Bayes filter (Chapter 3) and it is used in two forms:

    • Density form: given xtx_txt​, utu_tut​, xt−1x_{t-1}xt−1​, return the number p(xt∣ut,xt−1)p(x_t \mid u_t, x_{t-1})p(xt​∣ut​,xt−1​). Needed by histogram and grid filters.
    • Sampling form: given utu_tut​ and xt−1x_{t-1}xt−1​, draw a random xtx_txt​. Needed by particle filters, and usually much easier to implement.

    4.2Probabilistic kinematics - concepts

    What probabilistic kinematics means

    Review question 1 asks you to explain probabilistic kinematics. The key statements:

    1. The motion of a real robot is subject to noise from wheel slip, uneven floors, tyre deformation, motor control errors and unmodelled dynamics.
    2. Instead of predicting one successor pose, we predict a probability distribution over successor poses.
    3. The model is stated for small time steps and planar rigid robots. It assumes the robot's dynamics are negligible (kinematic model), so controls are velocities or displacements, not forces.
    4. Two families exist, according to what utu_tut​ is: the velocity motion model (controls are commanded translational and rotational velocities) and the odometry motion model (controls are odometry measurements from encoders).
    5. Noise parameters α1…α6\alpha_1 \ldots \alpha_6α1​…α6​ are robot-specific and are learned or tuned.

    Algorithms for sampling from distributions

    Sampling motion models need random numbers with a specified variance. Two standard samplers for zero mean and variance b2b^2b2:

    sample_normal(b2)=b6∑i=112rand(−1,1)\text{sample\_normal}(b^2) = \frac{b}{6}\sum_{i=1}^{12}\text{rand}(-1, 1)sample_normal(b2)=6b​i=1∑12​rand(−1,1)
    sample_triangular(b2)=b 62(rand(−1,1)+rand(−1,1))\text{sample\_triangular}(b^2) = b\,\frac{\sqrt6}{2}\big(\text{rand}(-1,1) + \text{rand}(-1,1)\big)sample_triangular(b2)=b26​​(rand(−1,1)+rand(−1,1))

    The sum of 12 uniform variables on [−1,1][-1, 1][−1,1] has variance 12×1/3=412 \times 1/3 = 412×1/3=4, so dividing by 6 and multiplying by bbb gives variance b2b^2b2; by the central limit theorem it is close to Gaussian. The corresponding density function prob(a,b2)\text{prob}(a, b^2)prob(a,b2) returns the zero-mean normal (or triangular) density of aaa.

    Notation
    Throughout this chapter, prob(a,b2)\text{prob}(a, b^2)prob(a,b2) is a density of error aaa with variance b2b^2b2, and sample(b2)\text{sample}(b^2)sample(b2) draws from it.

    4.3Probabilistic kinematics - examples, use cases, problems

    What the uncertainty looks like: the banana

    If a robot starts from a known pose and executes a motion, the distribution of successor poses is not an ellipse. Heading error at the start of the motion is amplified into lateral error along the path, so the distribution bends into the characteristic banana shape. The effect grows with distance and with rotational noise.

    Use case: wheel odometry in warehouses
    Warehouse AMRs drive long straight aisles. Odometry alone, typically accurate to 1-2 percent of distance, accumulates tens of centimetres of lateral error over a 30 m aisle, enough to hit racking. That is why every such robot fuses odometry with LiDAR scan matching, floor fiducials or ceiling landmarks.

    Problems and design choices

    Choosing and using a motion model raises recurring problems:

    • Velocity or odometry? Odometry is usually more accurate because it measures what the wheels did rather than what was commanded, but it is available only after the motion, so it cannot be used for planning.
    • Time step. Both models assume constant controls over Δt\Delta tΔt; long steps with changing speeds violate this.
    • Tuning α\alphaα: too small and the filter becomes overconfident and diverges; too large and it wastes particles. Tune by driving known paths (UMBmark squares) and matching spread.
    • Mobile manipulators (review question 7): a mobile robotic arm's end-effector location is the base pose composed with the arm's forward kinematics, WTE=WTB(xt) BTE(q){}^W T_E = {}^W T_B(x_t)\,{}^B T_E(q)WTE​=WTB​(xt​)BTE​(q). Base pose uncertainty from the motion model propagates to the gripper and grows with arm reach, which is why mobile pick-and-place robots re-localise the object with a wrist camera before grasping.

    4.4Velocity motion model - concepts

    Controls as velocities, motion as arcs

    The velocity motion model assumes the robot is controlled through a translational velocity vtv_tvt​ and a rotational velocity ωt\omega_tωt​: ut=(vt,ωt)Tu_t = (v_t, \omega_t)^Tut​=(vt​,ωt​)T. Differential-drive, synchro-drive and skid-steer robots are commonly commanded this way (a ROS cmd_vel message). If both are held constant for Δt\Delta tΔt, the robot moves on a circle of radius r=∣v/ω∣r = |v/\omega|r=∣v/ω∣ about the centre

    xc=x−vωsin⁡θ,yc=y+vωcos⁡θ,x_c = x - \frac{v}{\omega}\sin\theta,\qquad y_c = y + \frac{v}{\omega}\cos\theta,xc​=x−ωv​sinθ,yc​=y+ωv​cosθ,

    and the noise-free successor pose is

    (x′y′θ′)=(xyθ)+(−vωsin⁡θ+vωsin⁡(θ+ωΔt)vωcos⁡θ−vωcos⁡(θ+ωΔt)ωΔt).\begin{pmatrix}x'\\y'\\\theta'\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\\theta\end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix}-\frac{v}{\omega}\sin\theta + \frac{v}{\omega}\sin(\theta + \omega\Delta t)\\ \frac{v}{\omega}\cos\theta - \frac{v}{\omega}\cos(\theta + \omega\Delta t)\\ \omega\Delta t\end{pmatrix}.​x′y′θ′​​=​xyθ​​+​−ωv​sinθ+ωv​sin(θ+ωΔt)ωv​cosθ−ωv​cos(θ+ωΔt)ωΔt​​.

    Adding noise, and why a third parameter gamma is needed

    The executed velocities are the commanded ones plus zero-mean noise whose variance grows with the commands:

    (v^ω^)=(vω)+(εα1v2+α2ω2εα3v2+α4ω2).\begin{pmatrix}\hat v\\ \hat\omega\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}v\\ \omega\end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{\alpha_1 v^2 + \alpha_2\omega^2}\\ \varepsilon_{\alpha_3 v^2 + \alpha_4\omega^2}\end{pmatrix}.(v^ω^​)=(vω​)+(εα1​v2+α2​ω2​εα3​v2+α4​ω2​​).

    α1,α2\alpha_1, \alpha_2α1​,α2​ govern translational error, α3,α4\alpha_3, \alpha_4α3​,α4​ rotational error. With only (v^,ω^)(\hat v, \hat\omega)(v^,ω^) the successor pose would always lie exactly on some circle, so the final heading would be determined by the position: the distribution would live on a 2D surface inside 3D pose space, which is degenerate. The model therefore adds a final rotation γ^\hat\gammaγ^​ with variance α5v2+α6ω2\alpha_5 v^2 + \alpha_6\omega^2α5​v2+α6​ω2:

    θ′=θ+ω^Δt+γ^Δt.\theta' = \theta + \hat\omega\Delta t + \hat\gamma\Delta t .θ′=θ+ω^Δt+γ^​Δt.

    Applications and limitations of the velocity model

    Review question 3 and ISA-2 question 1b ask for the applications of the velocity motion model:

    • Prediction in filters when no odometry exists, for example legged robots, drones or simulated robots where only commands are known.
    • Motion planning and control: because it uses commands, it can predict the outcome of a candidate command before executing it. The dynamic window approach and model-predictive controllers roll it forward over candidate (v,ω)(v, \omega)(v,ω) pairs.
    • Particle-filter localization and FastSLAM in their original formulations.
    • Collision checking of the swept arc during the next control period.
    Limitations
    Real robots cannot change velocity instantly, so the constant-velocity-per-step assumption is violated during accelerations. Commanded velocities differ from executed ones because of motor controllers and load; odometry captures the executed motion better. The model has singular behaviour as ω→0\omega \to 0ω→0, which code must handle.

    4.6Odometry motion model - concepts

    Using odometry as a measurement of the control

    Most wheeled robots integrate wheel-encoder counts into an odometry pose estimate xˉt\bar x_txˉt​ in their own internal frame, which drifts relative to the world. The odometry motion model uses only the relative motion between two successive odometry poses, ut=(xˉt−1,xˉt)Tu_t = (\bar x_{t-1}, \bar x_t)^Tut​=(xˉt−1​,xˉt​)T, which is accurate over short intervals even though the absolute odometry drifts. The relative motion is decomposed into an initial rotation, a translation and a final rotation:

    δrot1=atan2⁡(yˉ′−yˉ, xˉ′−xˉ)−θˉ,δtrans=(xˉ−xˉ′)2+(yˉ−yˉ′)2,δrot2=θˉ′−θˉ−δrot1.\delta_{rot1} = \operatorname{atan2}(\bar y' - \bar y,\ \bar x' - \bar x) - \bar\theta,\qquad \delta_{trans} = \sqrt{(\bar x - \bar x')^2 + (\bar y - \bar y')^2},\qquad \delta_{rot2} = \bar\theta' - \bar\theta - \delta_{rot1}.δrot1​=atan2(yˉ​′−yˉ​, xˉ′−xˉ)−θˉ,δtrans​=(xˉ−xˉ′)2+(yˉ​−yˉ​′)2​,δrot2​=θˉ′−θˉ−δrot1​.

    Real-life example
    The Nav2 AMCL node exposes exactly the odometry model's four parameters as alpha1 to alpha4 (plus alpha5 for omnidirectional bases), and the documentation recommends tuning them by watching the particle cloud during rotations and straight runs, the same experiment as the tool above.
